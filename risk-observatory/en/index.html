Full situation briefing · 5 November 2026

As of 5 November 2026, 101 events are documented across eight dimensions; the figures show documented and verified cases, not overall frequency. Since 4 November, no assessment has changed materially; three events in offensive cyber are newly documented but were already public on 4 November and do not indicate a new development. The evidence is often thin: many cases rest on vendor statements or single sources without independent confirmation, and 29 events come from controlled experiments. For persistent memory there is only a single lab report, and for self-modification and shutdown evasion two cases each without demonstrated real-world impact.

Positive developments in safety measures

  • ImplementedAnthropic's IPO filing says its own threshold for AI-accelerated development may be near

    According to its amended IPO prospectus (S-1/A), internal estimates and expectations for upcoming models suggest Anthropic may soon reach its Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for “dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI”; no current model is designated above it. Strong acceleration has so far been noticed only in hindsight. Anthropic does not expect Claude to safely build future frontier models autonomously without meaningful human oversight; policy changes are under review.

  • In forceOpenAI updates the Model Spec around five core values

    According to OpenAI, the Model Spec, its public description of expected model behavior, was updated on 29 October 2026 and organized around five values: faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. New is guidance on context, oversight and agency; much of the detailed style guidance is retired. What is documented is the publication, as OpenAI's own statement, not adherence by the models.

  • ImplementedAnthropic gives METR access to internal systems

    According to Anthropic (15 October 2026), six employees of the independent organisation METR have already been onboarded with company hardware, office space and access to internal tools and systems roughly on par with Anthropic’s own risk assessment teams. Anthropic cannot veto publication but can require redaction of information in predefined categories (e.g. confidential IP). Self-reported by Anthropic only.
