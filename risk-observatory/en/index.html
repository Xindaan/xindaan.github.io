Full situation briefing · 27 October 2026

As of 27 Oct 2026, 66 events have been published; the figures show documented, verified cases, not overall frequency. For offensive cyber, risk and documented frequency rise: reports describe real attacks using AI agents, mostly from individual security firms without independent confirmation; for evaluator evasion and unauthorized tool use the evidence broadens while the risk rating stays the same. These changes largely stem from added reports that were still missing from the assessment of 26 Oct. Evidence remains thin for persistent memory, self-modification and shutdown evasion: single sources, lab settings or self-reports. Corrected version of 27 October 2026: extended by events from a catch-up review.

    South Korea: police arrest alleged leader who breached over 90 organisations with the AI pentest suite ARTEX

    According to BleepingComputer, the Seoul Metropolitan Police identified seven suspects of a group that breached more than 90 organisations in South Korea and stole about 8.2 million records. According to the police, the AI pentest suite ARTEX was used in most attacks.

    21 October 2026
    EV-0086
    Microsoft: Storm-0940 used AI framework ARTEX in the ConnectWise supply chain attack

    According to Microsoft, the group Storm-0940 used the open-source framework ARTEX in the supply chain attack on a multi-tenant ConnectWise application for reconnaissance, password spraying and OAuth abuse at downstream victims. Wiz Research independently observed an ARTEX-using actor with overlaps.

    Misuse by humansEvidence quality: Moderate
  3. First reported
    21 October 2026
    EV-0101
    CrowdStrike: four threat actors use Claude Code in attack frameworks

    According to CrowdStrike, four previously unreported threat actors left their offensive agent frameworks exposed in open directories; all four used Claude Code. According to CrowdStrike, at least 15 healthcare entities in Argentina were compromised, among others, and data was exfiltrated from at least two targets.

Positive developments in safety measures

  • ImplementedAnthropic gives METR access to internal systems

    According to Anthropic (15 October 2026), six employees of the independent organisation METR have already been onboarded with company hardware, office space and access to internal tools and systems roughly on par with Anthropic’s own risk assessment teams. Anthropic cannot veto publication but can require redaction of information in predefined categories (e.g. confidential IP). Self-reported by Anthropic only.

  • Partially implementedEU Commission: information requests on loss-of-control incidents

    According to a European Commission notice on an extraordinary meeting of its scientific AI panel (12 October 2026), the panel worked with the AI Office on questions for the companies that developed the models involved. The AI Office has already requested and received serious incident reports and sent information requests on safety practices; further requests to the companies involved have been announced. The source names neither the companies nor specific incidents.

  • ImplementedAnthropic: expanded Cyber Verification Program with tiered access

    According to Anthropic, the Cyber Verification Program was expanded with risk-tiered access on 6 October 2026. On CyScenarioBench the safeguards blocked 46 of 50 tasks on Claude Opus 5.5 in the Defense Access tier; the Red Team Access tier blocked none, and Opus 5.5 completed 34 of 50 (the same as with no safeguards). For the Specialized Access tier Anthropic currently reviews every organization in depth in collaboration with the US government. Self-reported only.
