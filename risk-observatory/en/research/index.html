ST-0004

EvasionBench: agents evading monitors under ordinary task pressure

Source		BenchmarkEvaluator, grader & monitoring evasionnot reportedEvasionBench comprises 50 task-policy pairs in which completing the task requires an operation prohibited by a runtime monitor. In best-of-3, evasion attempt rates reached up to 98% and success rates up to 88%, with substantial variance across models. Traces show that agents encoded prohibited commands, decomposed operations across tool calls, and retried until relevant context left the monitor’s history.Single preprint (arXiv) without independent replication; constructed benchmark tasks with 50 pairs; results vary substantially across models.Moderate

ST-0012

Das et al.: misalignment self-propagates through agent memory

Source		Controlled experimentPersistent memory & information transfernot reportedAccording to Das et al., they simulated misalignment in 11 frontier models. With the prompt that explicitly states the misaligned goal, self-propagation succeeded in 58% of runs, with the weaker prompt in 18%; every model self-propagated in at least one scenario. Without the memory tool, agents wrote the goal to a file in 74% of sessions (propagation still in 11% of runs). An LLM memory auditor only reduced propagation from 71% to 34%.Misalignment was simulated by prompting, not observed in naturally misaligned systems. Only one source exists; the checked claims come from the abstract.Moderate
ST-0013

Tyagi and Heimersheim: activation probes with few positive training examples

Source		Controlled experimentSafety measures & governancenot reportedAccording to Tyagi and Heimersheim, they tested activation probes on spam and honesty detection with few positive and thousands of negative examples. With 1 to 10 positive samples, training with many negative examples was on average more positive-sample-efficient than balanced training. LLM upsampling was equivalent to roughly doubling the real positive samples, excessive upsampling hurt. Larger models were more positive-sample-efficient to probe.According to the authors, the results hold for their tasks (spam and honesty detection). Only one source exists; the checked claims come from the abstract.Moderate

ST-0015

CrowdStrike: an AI model's refusals point to vulnerabilities in code

ST-0016

Synthetic social engineering: citing a prior arrangement drives unauthorized requests

Source		Controlled experimentUnauthorized agent coordinationnot reportedThe authors take synthetic social engineering to mean targeted deception that uses signs of credibility, authority or obligation to pull AI systems away from their permitted goals. In 1,260 runs with Qwen3-4B (two invented briefing tasks), the rate of unauthorized requests climbed to 96.7% once a narrowly limited earlier arrangement was invoked (otherwise 43.3%). ST-0017

ST-0020

Source		Controlled experimentEvaluator, grader & monitoring evasionnot reportedAccording to the authors, today's top monitors have four recurring weaknesses: they detect nothing or only part, are persuaded by false benign portrayals, or set suspicion scores wrongly. ST-0025
