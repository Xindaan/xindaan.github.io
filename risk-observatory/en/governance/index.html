  • GV-0001StandardUnknown

    China: AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0 published

    Jurisdiction
    China
    Announced
    14 September 2026
    Implementation
    Unknown

    Evidence of implementation: The National Technical Committee for Cybersecurity Standardization (TC260) published the “AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0” on 14 September 2026 (announced on the CAC site). According to a summary it addresses, among others, excessive agent permissions, unexpected tool use, persistent memory, autonomous cyberattacks and loss of control, and proposes, among others, agent identities, least-privilege rights and controlled shutdown.

    Source: 《人工智能安全治理框架3.0》发布_中央网络安全和信息化委员会办公室
  • GV-0002StandardAnnounced

    China: loss of control as an agent risk and agent safety standard

    Jurisdiction
    China
    Announced
    May 2026
    Implementation
    Announced

    Evidence of implementation: According to Reuters (as republished by the Kathmandu Post on 15 September 2026), a policy issued in May 2026 by China’s cyberspace regulator, economic planner and industry ministry identifies “operational loss of control” as a security risk for AI agents. According to the report, China is working on a mandatory national agent safety standard; its completion or entry into force is not documented.

    Source: How China is preparing for the risk of AI escaping human control
  • GV-0003Voluntary commitmentImplemented

    OpenAI: framework for reporting model misalignment

    Jurisdiction
    OpenAI (Selbstverpflichtung)
    Announced
    16 September 2026
    Implementation
    Implemented

    Evidence of implementation: According to OpenAI, the company introduced a formal process for reporting model misalignment and writes that the AI industry has not solved alignment and monitoring sufficiently to continue responsibly scaling at maximum speed for much longer. According to OpenAI, misalignment monitoring now runs on 100% of training samples (previously 20%), cross-sample communication counts as a P0 incident, and live internet access in training is disabled globally. Self-reported.

    Source: Our framework for reporting model misalignment | OpenAI
  • GV-0004Independent auditAnnounced

    Anthropic and Accenture: embedded independent evaluation

    Jurisdiction
    Anthropic mit Accenture/Faculty (freiwillige Partnerschaft)
    Announced
    18 September 2026
    Implementation
    Announced

    Evidence of implementation: According to Anthropic, the company announced a partnership with Accenture/Faculty on 18 September 2026 for embedded, independent evaluation of frontier AI. Unlike today’s external evaluators, embedded evaluators are to work inside AI companies, with access comparable to an employee’s. Anthropic and Accenture each expect to invest at least $1 billion over the next five years; many details of the model are still open.

    Source: Partnering with Accenture on embedded evaluation \ Anthropic
  • GV-0005RegulationIn force

    California: Executive Order N-9-26 on frontier AI oversight

    Jurisdiction
    Kalifornien
    Announced
    18 September 2026
    Implementation
    In force

    Evidence of implementation: On 18 September 2026 California used Executive Order N-9-26 to accelerate existing statutory deadlines for frontier AI oversight. By 16 November 2026 it requires recommendations only: on the feasibility of independent on-site assessors in frontier labs and external verification of safety frameworks and risk reports, on the definition of reportable “critical safety incidents” (including loss of control), and on the feasibility and legal basis of a “kill switch”. A kill switch is not mandated.

    Source: https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FINAL-N-9-26-AI-EO-9.18.26-SIGNED.pdf

  • GV-0007Voluntary commitmentImplemented

    OpenAI holds back GPT-6.1 Astra after it missed safety standards

    Jurisdiction
    OpenAI (interne Freigabeentscheidung)
    Announced
    September 2026
    Implementation
    Implemented

    Evidence of implementation: According to WIRED (29 September 2026), OpenAI cancelled the release of GPT-6.1 Astra planned for the following month after the model failed to meet safety standards. According to head of safety systems Saachi Jain, it narrowly missed the bar on scope, authorization and communicating back to the user about the work done. For GPT-6 Astra, OpenAI reported 0% overshoots of the authorized target in its own evaluation (GPT-5.6 Sol without production safeguards: 48%).

    Source: OpenAI Delays Release of Latest Model Over Safety Concerns | WIRED
  • GV-0008Voluntary commitmentPartially implemented

    OpenAI: safety cases for frontier AI training

    Jurisdiction
    OpenAI (Selbstverpflichtung)
    Announced
    28 September 2026
    Implementation
    Partially implemented

    Evidence of implementation: In a post dated 28 September 2026 OpenAI proposes formal safety cases for frontier RL training runs, including dissents, multiple veto rights, fail-closed monitoring, automatic run pauses, audits and incident postmortems; the source phrases these as recommendations (“should”/“could”). According to OpenAI they are being implemented at OpenAI and are expected to evolve over the coming weeks. The text does not state that they are mandatory or independent.

    Source: Towards safety cases for frontier AI training | OpenAI

  • GV-0011Voluntary commitmentImplemented

    Anthropic: expanded Cyber Verification Program with tiered access

    Jurisdiction
    Anthropic (Selbstverpflichtung)
    Announced
    6 October 2026
    Implementation
    Implemented

    Evidence of implementation: According to Anthropic, the Cyber Verification Program was expanded with risk-tiered access on 6 October 2026. On CyScenarioBench the safeguards blocked 46 of 50 tasks on Claude Opus 5.5 in the Defense Access tier; the Red Team Access tier blocked none, and Opus 5.5 completed 34 of 50 (the same as with no safeguards).   • GV-0012RegulationAnnounced

    White House: reporting requirement for AI security incidents

    Jurisdiction
    USA (Weißes Haus)
    Announced
    9 October 2026
    Implementation

    Evidence of implementation: According to SemiAnalysis, Chinese safety frameworks, including Framework 3.0, explicitly address unauthorised resource use, evaluator deception, sandbox escapes and loss of control, among others with a case study of models disabling shutdown scripts, sandbagging under evaluation and breaking out of isolation.
