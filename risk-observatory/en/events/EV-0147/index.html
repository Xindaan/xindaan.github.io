Google: attackers operate agentic frameworks such as ARTEX in real intrusions

According to the Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG), GTIG and Mandiant have observed since late August 2026 threat actors operating open-source multi-agent pentesting platforms such as ARTEX, PentAGI and CAIRN within Mandiant incident response engagements. Agentic activity carried out significant parts of the attack lifecycle.

Observed behaviour
According to GTIG, several threat actors used ARTEX for post-compromise reconnaissance, lateral movement and privilege escalation, including financially motivated actors for data-theft extortion and suspected PRC-nexus clusters. In several cases the attackers supplied the framework with compromised credentials of the target organization and instructed it to execute the subsequent attack stages. Execution ran in compressed bursts of roughly 2 to 25 seconds, once 27 events in 23 seconds while probing AWS permissions. In one case ARTEX executed SQL commands via an exposed Weaver OA web server and wrote several webshells until they ran cleanly.
Demonstrated consequences
According to GTIG, financially motivated actors used ARTEX to facilitate data-theft extortion; in one case the framework executed SQL commands on a web server with valid credentials and got webshells running. Affected organizations and the extent of the damage are not stated.
Severity

Attribution
According to GTIG and Mandiant, attributed from their own engagements to the named frameworks, operated by human threat actors; individual actors are only described as suspected PRC-nexus or financially motivated. (Attribution confidence: High)

Rationale of the assessment
The primary source is the GTIG and Mandiant report on their own investigations; they are neither vendor nor affected party. It is the only source, victims and details are not public; minor gaps.

Alternative explanations

  • Part of the activity could stem from human-driven tooling; GTIG describes the sequences as agentic.

Methodological limitations

  • Single source; no independent confirmation.
  • Affected organizations and the number of engagements are not stated; observations come from incident responses, not a full survey.
  • Google itself sells security services (interest).
