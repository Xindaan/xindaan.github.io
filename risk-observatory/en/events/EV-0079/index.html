NorForge: exposed Cairn control panels reveal AI-driven attack operations

On 19 October 2026 NorForge published an investigation of publicly exposed control panels of AI-driven penetration-testing frameworks, including Cairn, which NorForge says are extensively used in malicious cyber operations. The largest observed cluster comprises over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards; the success rate is about 10%.

Observed behaviour
Cairn is a multi-agent framework: agents post every fact onto a project-internal blackboard to which human operators can also add hints; the data is available to every agent. In the largest cluster (AIA0001, Chinese-language, over 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards), successful projects took on average a little under 23 hours to reach the goal. NorForge estimates model cost at about 5 US dollars per task but has no actual token numbers. One task explicitly demanded a webshell be installed in order to reach the goal.
Demonstrated consequences
NorForge checked one claimed success directly: the URL named in a completed task, aimed at a large hardware parts vendor, did serve an uploader webshell. Further compromises are not independently confirmed; the roughly 10% success rate rests on heterogeneous tasks and in part on the agents' self-reports.

The Reuters text could not be retrieved, and independent verification by Reuters is not documented. Final rating after cross-check and fixed rules: moderate.

Methodological limitations

  • Success rate and task counts come from exposed panels, partly from the agents' self-reports; only one success was verified directly.
  • Not documented and not adopted: start date of 25 September, “over 1,200” websites, “more than 100” compromises, total cost of about 5,000 US dollars, instructions under 100 characters, and the victims the legacy report attributes to Reuters.
  • Independent verification of the results by Reuters is not in the text; Reuters is only named as a cooperation partner in notifying victims.
