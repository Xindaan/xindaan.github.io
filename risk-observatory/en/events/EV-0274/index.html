Earn-then-Exploit: trust-building web fraud succeeds on coding agents at 71.4 percent

According to the authors, their attack reached an average 71.4 percent success on three coding agents across 56 constructed organizational environments, 85.7 percent on Codex-GPT-5.5.

Observed behaviour
According to the authors, the EtE (Earn-then-Exploit) attack first builds credibility through a web source and exploits it later. Across 56 environments (8 task contexts x 7 attack consequences), scene-level success was 85.7 percent on Codex-GPT-5.5, 67.9 on ZCode-GLM-5.3-Flash and 60.7 percent on QwenCode-Qwen3.7-Plus, well above all injection-based baselines. The authors report that the agents showed trust-like reliance on previously helpful web sources. The proposed action-authorization separation reduced the success rate to 0 percent.

Alternative explanations

  • The success rates depend on the constructed scenarios and the design of the attack.

Methodological limitations

  • Only the abstract was checked; construction of the environments is not individually verified.
  • The authors evaluate their own attack and defense (conflict of interest).
  • Controlled test, no verified harm to third parties.
