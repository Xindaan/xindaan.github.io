PHP2Shell: AI-assisted PHP flaws, followed by real attacks up to a wiper

According to Wiz, it found the first of two critical PHP flaws (up to CVSS 9.2) with frontier models; OpenAI says its models found the flaw. After a leak, Cloudflare and Wiz saw attack attempts; Check Point Research reports intrusions via unpatched gateways, with destruction at some Israeli organizations. According to Decrypt, three countries took government websites offline.

Observed behaviour
According to Wiz, Wiz Research discovered the first of the two PHP flaws on 19 August 2026 using Wiz Atlas, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol. According to OpenAI, the flaw was found with OpenAI models under Patch the Planet; according to Decrypt, Calif found a version in August with GPT-5.6 Sol. According to Cloudflare, the flaw leaked before the release date, followed by attack traffic with mutated payloads. According to Check Point Research, from 30 October an attacker used PHP2Shell as initial access on unpatched gateways, escalated via CVE-2026-123039 and deployed the wiper destroy_v6 on an ESXi host. Evidence of AI use by the attackers is missing.
Demonstrated consequences
According to Decrypt, Finland, the Netherlands and France took government websites offline because of the flaws. According to Cloudflare there was attack traffic against its customers. According to Wiz, a honeypot was successfully exploited, while Wiz saw no successful full exploitation in its own telemetry through late October. According to Check Point Research, the first wave on 30 October targeted Israeli organizations and included destructive activity against some; in the investigated intrusion the wiper ran on an ESXi host. A second wave on 2 November hit organizations in several countries with no evidence of destruction. The extent of deleted data and the number of victims are not established.
Severity
High
Attribution
Wiz explicitly attributes the discovery of the first flaw to Wiz Atlas, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol (with a timeline), and OpenAI attributes the discovery to its own models; both are statements by the discoverers. Who is behind the gateway intrusions is not established, nor is any AI use by the attackers. (Attribution confidence: High)
Independent confirmation
Partial
Rationale of the assessment
Primary sources from Wiz, Cloudflare, the Canadian Cyber Centre and Check Point Research establish the flaws and their consequences. Successful intrusions up to the wiper rest on Check Point Research alone; Cloudflare saw attack traffic, the Cyber Centre no active exploitation, Wiz no successful full exploitation in its own telemetry. The models' role rests on statements by the discoverers Wiz and OpenAI (via Decrypt); the extent of destruction is not quantified. Final rating after cross-check and fixed rules: moderate.

Alternative explanations

  • The flaws could also have been found without frontier models; the sources only show that models were used, not that they were necessary.

Methodological limitations

  • The models' role in discovery rests on statements by Wiz and OpenAI, who may have an interest in the framing; Decrypt relays the OpenAI statement.
  • The sources contain no evidence of AI use in the attacks themselves; the consequences stem from the vulnerabilities, not demonstrably from AI behavior.
  • The government website shutdowns rest on a single media source (Decrypt); details on the extent of destruction at victims are missing.
  • The Canadian Cyber Centre saw no active exploitation by 23 October and Wiz no successful full exploitation in its own telemetry by 27 October; the intrusions came later.
