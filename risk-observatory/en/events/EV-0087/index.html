PHP2Shell: AI-assisted discovery of PHP flaws, followed by real attacks up to a wiper

According to Wiz, Wiz Research used AI models to find the first of two critical PHP flaws; according to OpenAI, the flaw was discovered with its own models. After disclosure, according to Check Point Research, an attacker used it via unpatched gateways for intrusions, with destructive activity at some Israeli organizations.

Observed behaviour
According to Wiz, Wiz Research discovered the first flaw on 19 August 2026 using Wiz Atlas, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol and reported the flaws to the PHP security team on 24 August; according to OpenAI, it was discovered with OpenAI models as part of Patch the Planet. According to Cloudflare, the flaw was leaked before the official release date; Cloudflare observed attack traffic against its customers whose payloads were mutated to bypass protections. According to Check Point Research, from 30 October an attacker used the flaw via Mobile Access portals of unpatched Security Gateways for initial access, escalated privileges via a further flaw, pivoted over SSH to a VMware ESXi host in the investigated case and deployed the destroy_v6 wiper there.
Demonstrated consequences
According to Check Point Research, the first wave on 30 October targeted Israeli organizations and included destructive activity against some of them; a broader second wave on 2 November targeted organizations across multiple countries with no evidence of destruction. According to Decrypt, Finland, the Netherlands and France took government websites offline. According to CBS News, a NSA network outage on 17 November was linked to urgent patching of the flaws; according to US intelligence director Jay Clayton, some systems did not come back up as expected after patching. The sources do not quantify the extent or nature of the destruction.

Attribution
The discovery with AI models rests on self-reports by Wiz and OpenAI (medium). According to Check Point Research, the attacks are attributed to an unnamed attacker; a role of AI in the attacks is not documented. (Attribution confidence: Medium)

Rationale of the assessment
Several independent sources support the core: the reports by Wiz and Cloudflare, Check Point Research's own investigation, the Canadian Cyber Centre, and coverage by Decrypt, CBS News and Fox Business. The role of AI in the discovery rests on Wiz and OpenAI themselves. Final rating after cross-check and fixed rules: moderate.

Alternative explanations

  • The flaw could also have been found without AI support; it is only documented that AI models were used in the discovery, not that they were necessary.
  • The NSA network outage is linked to patching, not to an attack.

Methodological limitations

  • The role of AI in the discovery rests on self-reports by Wiz and OpenAI, who have an interest in the framing.
  • The intrusion with the wiper is documented only by Check Point Research and in detail only for one investigated case; the extent of destruction and number of affected organizations are open.
  • In its own telemetry Wiz saw only a limited number of exploitation attempts and no successful end-to-end exploitation until 27 October; according to Wiz, two scanners successfully exploited a researcher's honeypot. The attacks reported by Check Point Research began afterwards.
