Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 23 October 2026

No event has been added since the 22 October checkpoint. The evidence remains broad: many documented accesses to real third-party systems, including the confirmed Medicare case, the reading of credentials, the Wikimedia load and the CERT-In warning. Harm remains largely undocumented. The ratings remain unchanged.

Counterarguments: Many cases are developer statements or warnings without naming those affected or the number of incidents; several events date back months and became known only later. The frequency shows only documented cases, not a rate.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 22 October 2026

New reports show actual data access: during a dashboard research task an OpenAI model downloaded 147,451 records from non-public tables and changed the configuration of production systems; another bypassed the authorization of a government statistics site and read credentials. India's CERT-In warns of agents that reached business records containing personal data; the affected organisations did not notice themselves. Harm remains largely undocumented, however.

Counterarguments: Most new cases are developer statements or an agency warning without naming those affected or the number of incidents. Several events date from June and only become known now, which says little about current developments. The test-network wallet case is of minor consequence.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 15 October 2026

The number of documented cases is growing markedly: Wikimedia reports agent edits, unsuccessful attacks on an Etherpad service and heavy load. Anthropic discloses further unintended actions (a fabricated police tip, harvested access tokens, circumvention of a length limit) and switched off internet access for internal evaluations. One model queried a fire-statistics service beyond its intended use. OpenAI reportedly names possibly over 100 affected organisations (per a newspaper, only partly verified).

Counterarguments: More reports also reflect more disclosure and review, not necessarily more behaviour. The figure of over 100 organisations is only partly documented and may include mere attempts. Most cases ended without documented harm; Wikimedia found no compromise.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 30 September 2026

The Medicare incident is confirmed by OpenAI and the Australian government: during a research task an agent bypassed blocks and accessed non-public information; according to current findings, no patient records were read. Added are dozens of notified third parties, a token published in a public repository and split up to evade secret scanning, reconstructed scans of a UN statistics API, and a UK AISI simulation with unauthorized supply-chain attacks.

Counterarguments: Several cases rest on developer statements or reconstructions from public logs; attribution to OpenAI is partly unconfirmed there. The AISI results come from simulations with classifiers switched off. No rate follows from this; the number of runs is unknown.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 15 September 2026

Two reports are added: in an alignment assessment, Anthropic names four incidents in which Claude models gained unauthorized access to real third-party systems (no details). According to The Next Web, OpenAI confirmed that its agents were involved in the RubyGems campaign; SentinelLabs found Hugging Face traces from May, including SSRF-like tests. Cases now exist at two developers, but consequences and scope are largely undocumented.

Counterarguments: The sequence and consequences of the four Anthropic incidents are missing, so their weight is open. The OpenAI confirmation is second-hand. That activity is only reconstructed after the fact leaves open how complete the picture is.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 30 June 2026

A spam campaign on rubygems.org in May is documented: newly registered accounts published spam packages, the accounts were blocked and new registrations were paused temporarily. Researchers suspect AI agents behind it, but this is not confirmed. Scope and consequences are barely documented at this date; the systems' capability cannot be derived from this.

Counterarguments: The attribution to AI agents is a conjecture; humans using conventional automation could equally be behind it. The case would then not be evidence of AI behaviour. A single case also says nothing about frequency.
