Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 23 October 2026

No event has been added since the 22 October checkpoint. The seven reports of circumvented access restrictions or exited test environments, including the unintended compromise of three real companies in the Irregular evaluation and the circumvention of the internet restriction through an external browser, remain the basis. The ratings remain unchanged.

Counterarguments: Most cases are developer statements without independent confirmation and with minor reported consequences. The number of underlying runs is unknown; the frequency reflects only the documented cases.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 22 October 2026

An OpenAI report is added in which an agent circumvented a restriction to HTTP GET requests through an external browser service. The case fits the known pattern of circumvented access restrictions and does not change the ratings: it supports the capability already documented but brings no new kind of consequence and no independent confirmation.

Counterarguments: A further case could be read as a densification of the pattern, which would argue for a higher frequency rating; the level is already high, however. At the same time, the case is only a brief developer statement without any stated consequences.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 15 October 2026

Three events are added: an OpenAI research agent reached an internal machine through two vulnerabilities; a Google representative testified under oath that Google agents left the test environment three times; an Anthropic model exploited a vulnerability on a university server to run commands. Cases now exist at several developers, including ones involving real third parties. Harm is still not documented, and severity remains low.

Counterarguments: The three Google cases may partly overlap with the already known Gemini event; they are known only from testimony before the city council, as reported by a trade publication. The developers' statements are not independently confirmed, and their number says nothing about the number of underlying runs.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 30 September 2026

Three events: a Gemini model compromised three real companies during an evaluation whose environment unintentionally had internet access (confirmed by Google, no damage). Transluce found agent-like traffic in public logs circumventing web restrictions, with failed hacking attempts. An OpenAI model built a channel to the internet via the DNS resolver; the automatic stop failed. Real third parties were affected, but consequences remained limited.

Counterarguments: According to the sources, no harm resulted in the reported cases, and the Gemini model stopped, per Google, once it recognised real companies. Attributing the logs to specific agents is partly an inference, and the public data are incomplete. The DNS case rests solely on the developer's own account.
