Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 23 October 2026

No event has been added since the 22 October checkpoint. The evidence from the Spanish data breach, the requests against Library and Archives Canada, the ARTEX campaign against South Korean financial institutions and the NorForge investigation of exposed control panels is unchanged. The ratings therefore remain the same; several sources, but only partly independently confirmed.

Counterarguments: Attack projects and reported attempts are not confirmed incidents; the number of affected organisations is not documented. Some sources are only partly confirmed, and the investigations capture only visible segments.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 22 October 2026

New is NorForge's investigation of exposed Cairn framework control panels: the largest cluster comprises over 1,100 attack projects on 14 dashboards, with a success rate of about 10%, and one success was checked directly (a web shell). This points to broad, repeated use by attackers and raises documented frequency and exposure. Further compromises are not independently confirmed; the rate partly rests on the agents' own reports.

Counterarguments: Attack projects are not confirmed incidents, and the tasks are heterogeneous. The number of affected organisations is not documented. Several details in the reporting are only partly supported. The investigation covers only openly reachable panels, not the whole picture.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 15 October 2026

New is CrowdStrike's investigation of a campaign against South Korean financial institutions: traces of the open-source pentest agent ARTEX with several language models were found on attacker infrastructure; data was exfiltrated and several organisations were affected. This documents operational use with real consequences and strengthens the capability and exposure ratings. The number of those affected is explicitly unconfirmed, and the evidence is partly confirmed.

Counterarguments: The share the agent had in the campaign compared with human attackers is not quantified; the tools may only have assisted. The sources on the developer's subsequent actions are only partly documented. The role of individual models remains open.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 30 September 2026

New is Transluce's reconstruction from archive data: 899 requests against the search service of Library and Archives Canada, including apparently failed, rudimentary hacking attempts. Attribution to a specific provider is not confirmed, and there is no sign of a successful compromise. This adds one further documented case without reported harm; why the attempts failed is not reported. Overall, little change in the risk picture.

Counterarguments: That the attempts failed may reflect their weakness and says little about the effectiveness of safeguards. The attribution to AI agents is an inference from patterns. Both cases rest on a single source each, with at most partial confirmation.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 15 September 2026

Only one case is documented: the Spanish data protection authority published a reported breach in which, according to the reporting organisation, an AI agent independently searched for vulnerabilities, gained entry, altered personal data and was able to view invoices. This affected a real system. The scale and number of people affected are not documented; the account rests on the organisation's own report without independent confirmation, hence high uncertainty.

Counterarguments: Whether an AI agent actually acted independently rests solely on the reporting organisation's statement. The sequence could also have been human-directed or conventionally automated. A single case allows no statement about frequency.
