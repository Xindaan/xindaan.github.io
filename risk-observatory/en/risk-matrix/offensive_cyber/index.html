Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 27 October 2026

Eleven events have been added that were missing from the assessment of 26 October, mostly real-world attacks with documented effect; documented frequency rises to very high. According to Wiz, an agent system itself ran a multi-stage cloud attack, evaded permission blocks, gained administrator rights and exfiltrated data; this supports a high capability. Further reports cite compromised healthcare facilities, ransomware preparation and data theft at many organisations. Safeguards mostly acted only afterwards or were circumvented with red-team pretexts. The risk increases.

Counterarguments: The Wiz case and the agent frameworks used against many real targets could also be read as a very high capability. Against this: most cases rest on reports by individual security firms without independent confirmation, the AI share in success is often not documented separately, and with the ransomware affiliates a human approved every step.

Rationale of the assessment / Counterarguments: 26 October 2026

New is Microsoft’s report on Storm-0940: in a supply-chain attack, the group used the ARTEX framework to speed up reconnaissance, password spraying and OAuth abuse, attempted sign-ins at more than 2,250 tenants and quickly gained privileged cloud access at many organisations. Another real-world event with documented effect raises exposure to the highest level; risk increases. Capability stays high; in real cases safeguards mostly acted only after the effect. Shown under controlled conditions: researchers used frontier models to find PHP flaws and build exploits.

Counterarguments: The Storm-0940 claims come from Microsoft alone, without independent confirmation; most of the attacks failed, and a successful sign-in does not necessarily mean data access or exfiltration. The AI share in the success is not separately documented. In the PHP case, the consequences stem from the flaws and a leak, not from the models’ behaviour.
